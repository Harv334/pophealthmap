About and privacy

PopHealth Map is an independent project run by Harvey Seville. It is not an NHS or government service, and it is not endorsed by the NHS, any council or any of the organisations whose data it shows.

The figures come from published official sources, which the methodology and technical notes list with their dates, while the way they are put together and presented is this project's own work.

Select any ward on the map to read a short summary, headed In plain English, of how it compares with the other wards in its LA and its region.

In short

  • The site sets no cookies and has no analytics, no trackers and no adverts. Its fonts and map code are served from this site, not from Google or a code library service.
  • The map runs in your browser, on data files downloaded from this site. Selecting areas, filtering, comparing and exporting all happen on your own device.
  • Three optional parts of the page do send something elsewhere: the Ask panel, postcode search and the background map. Each is described below.

The Ask panel

Please do not enter patient or personal information. Ask about places, not people: no names, NHS numbers, addresses or details of anyone's health or circumstances.

The Ask about this data panel answers questions in plain English. When you send a question:

  • Your question goes to a small server that this site runs on Cloudflare, which passes it on to Anthropic, and Anthropic's Claude model writes the answer.
  • To write the answer, the model asks your browser for the figures it needs. Your browser looks them up in the map's own data, which it has already downloaded from this site, and sends them back the same way.
  • Everything said earlier in the panel goes along with each new question, so that the model can follow the conversation, and closing or reloading the page clears it.
  • To keep to the limit of 10 questions a day, the server on Cloudflare records your IP address with a count of the questions asked today, and it deletes that record automatically after 48 hours.
  • If you never send a question, nothing is sent to that server or to Anthropic, and opening the panel on its own sends nothing.

Anthropic and Cloudflare handle what they receive under their own policies: Anthropic's privacy policy and Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Answers are written by an AI model, so while the figures in them come from the map's own data, the wording does not, and you should check anything that matters before acting on it.

Postcode search

When you type a postcode into the search box, that postcode, and nothing else, is sent to postcodes.io, a free postcode lookup service, to find where it is. Like any website, postcodes.io also sees your IP address. Other searches, such as a ward, a GP practice or a charity, are matched in your browser and are not sent anywhere.

A full postcode can cover a small number of homes. If you are working from someone's address, search by ward or neighbourhood name instead.

The background map

The street map behind the data is made of image tiles requested from CARTO, or from Esri if CARTO's tiles cannot be loaded. If you switch the background to Satellite, the imagery and its place names come from Esri instead. As with any web request, the tile provider sees your IP address and which part of the map you are looking at. Nothing from the map's own data layers is sent with those requests.

Hosting

The pages and data files are served by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and the addresses of the pages and files your browser asks for. See GitHub's privacy statement.

What is stored in your browser

A few display preferences are kept in your browser's own storage so that the map opens the way you left it. They stay on your device and are not sent anywhere, and clearing this site's data in your browser removes them.

Charity locations

The charities on the map and in the Directory come from the Charity Commission's register of charities, which publishes each charity's contact details. Many small charities give a trustee's home as their contact address, so for smaller charities the site shows only an approximate location and no street address.

Anyone can ask for a charity's record to be removed from this site by emailing contact@pophealth.uk with the charity's name or registered number. Changing the details on the register itself is done with the Charity Commission.

The data on the map

The statistics on the map are published figures for areas, such as wards and neighbourhoods, with the publishers' own rules for small numbers already applied. They are not records about individuals. GP practices, pharmacies, dentists and hospitals come from published NHS registers. The methodology and technical notes list every source.

Questions and requests

For a question about this page, a correction, or a removal request, email contact@pophealth.uk. If you are not happy with the reply, you can also contact the Information Commissioner's Office.
