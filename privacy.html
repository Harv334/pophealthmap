About and privacy

PopHealth Map is an independent project run by Harvey Seville. It is not a National Health Service (NHS) or government service. It does not have the official support of the NHS, any council or any of the organisations whose data it shows.

The figures come from published official sources, and the methodology and technical notes list these sources and their dates. The way the figures are put together and presented is this project's own work.

PopHealth Map shows official figures on health, deprivation and local services on one map of England. The areas go from whole local authorities (LAs) down to neighbourhoods of a few hundred homes. It is for people who plan, fund or campaign for local services, such as public health and NHS teams, councillors, charities and community groups. It is also for anyone who wants to understand their own area. When you select a ward, the panel opens with a short summary of how it compares with the other wards in its LA and its region.

Summary

  • The site sets no cookies and has no analytics, no trackers and no adverts. Its fonts and the code for the map come from this site, not from Google or another company that hosts code.
  • The map runs in your browser, on data files downloaded from this site. Selecting areas, filtering, comparing and exporting all happen on your own device.
  • There are 3 parts of the map that send something to another service. The background map does this whenever the map is open, because its street map or satellite images come from CARTO or Esri. Postcode search and the Ask panel send something only when you use them. Each is described below.

The Ask panel

Please do not enter patient or personal information. Ask about places, not people: no names, NHS numbers, addresses or details of anyone's health or circumstances.

The Ask about this data panel is available on the London map and on each region's map. It answers questions about the figures on the map. An artificial intelligence (AI) model writes the answers. The panel tells the model which region's map you have open, so that it answers about that region's areas. When you send a question:

  • Your question goes to this site's server, which is a small server that runs on Cloudflare. Then the server passes it to Anthropic's Claude model, which writes the reply.
  • To write the reply, the model asks your browser for the figures it needs. Your browser finds them in the map's own data, which it has already downloaded from this site. It sends those figures back the same way, through this site's server to Anthropic. The model never gets the rest of the data.
  • With each new question, the panel also sends the earlier questions, answers and figures, so that the model can follow the conversation. Closing or reloading the page clears the conversation from the panel.
  • You can ask 10 questions a day, and replying when the model asks you to narrow a question down does not use one up. A work network that many people share also has its own daily limit, so you may reach it before you have asked 10 questions. To keep to these limits, the panel stores a random number in your browser and sends it with each question. The number does not identify anything else. The server on Cloudflare records the number and your IP address, with a count of the questions and requests sent today. Your IP address (internet protocol address) is a number that identifies your internet connection. The server deletes those records automatically after 48 hours.
  • Nothing is sent to that server or to Anthropic until you send a question. Opening the panel does not send anything.

The Ask panel is on the London map and each region's map. On the England map, which shows every region at once, it is not shown, and nothing is sent.

You can read more in Anthropic's privacy policy and Cloudflare's privacy policy.

An AI model writes the answers. We tell it to use only the figures it finds in the map's own data. But it can still make mistakes, so please check anything important before you act on it.

Postcode search

When you type a postcode into the search box, your browser sends that postcode, and nothing else, to postcodes.io. This is a free service that finds where a postcode is. Like any website, postcodes.io also sees your IP address. Other searches, such as for a ward, a GP (family doctor) practice or a charity, happen in your browser and are not sent anywhere.

A full postcode can cover a small number of homes. If you start from a person's address, search by the name of the ward or neighbourhood instead.

The background map

The street map behind the data is made of small square images, called tiles. Your browser gets them from CARTO, or from Esri if CARTO's tiles do not load. If you change the background to Satellite, the satellite images and their place names come from Esri instead. As with any request on the web, the company that sends the tiles sees your IP address and which part of the map you are looking at. Your browser sends nothing from the map's own data with those requests.

Hosting

GitHub Pages hosts the pages and data files. Like any web host, GitHub gets your IP address and the addresses of the pages and files that your browser asks for. You can read GitHub's privacy statement.

What is stored in your browser

A few display settings are kept in your browser's own storage, so that the map opens the way you left it. They stay on your device and are not sent anywhere. If you clear this site's data in your browser, they are removed.

NameKept inPurpose
phTourSeenLocal storage Notes that the tour has been shown, so it does not start by itself on every visit.
phPanelCollapsedLocal storage Whether you hid the side panel on a wide screen.
phPanelWidthLocal storage The width you dragged the side panel to.
phMapControlsFoldedLocal storage Whether the map controls in the side panel are folded or open.
phAskUsedLocal storage Today's date and how many Ask questions this browser has used, so the panel can show how many are left. Your questions themselves are not stored.
ph-ldr-sweptSession storage That the map's opening animation has already played during this visit, so it does not play again on the next page. It is cleared when you close the tab.
ph-england-okSession storage That you chose to open the map of all of England, which is a large download, so the map does not ask again. It is cleared when you close the tab.

Charity locations

The charities on the map and in the Directory come from the Charity Commission's register of charities, which publishes each charity's contact details. Many small charities give the home of a trustee, one of the people who run the charity, as their contact address. So for smaller charities, the site shows only a rough location and no street address.

Anyone can ask for a charity's record to be removed from this site by emailing contact@pophealth.uk with the charity's name or registered number. To change the details on the register itself, contact the Charity Commission.

The data on the map

The statistics on the map are published figures for areas, such as wards and neighbourhoods. The publishers have already applied their own rules for small numbers, for example by not publishing a figure when the numbers are too small. The figures are not records about individual people. GP practices, pharmacies, dentists and hospitals come from published NHS registers. The methodology and technical notes list every source.

Your personal data and your rights

Harvey Seville is responsible for the personal data this site handles. The only personal data the site keeps itself is your IP address and the Ask panel's random browser number, with a count of the Ask questions sent from them. The site deletes this data after 48 hours. We keep it because we have a legitimate interest in sharing the Ask panel fairly and keeping its cost within what the project can pay.

Cloudflare and Anthropic process your question for us as service providers. Either may handle it outside the United Kingdom (UK), including in the United States, under the safeguards in their data processing terms. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, Anthropic does not use questions sent to its models this way to train them. It keeps them only for a limited time, mainly to check for misuse.

You can ask for a copy of any personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it, by emailing contact@pophealth.uk.

Using this site

PopHealth Map is provided free and as it is. The figures are published statistics about areas, collected here for planning, research and discussion. They are not medical advice and do not describe any individual person. We take care to copy each source correctly, but we cannot promise that every figure is complete or up to date. We are not responsible for decisions that people make using them. You may reuse the figures under the licences and with the credits listed on the methodology page, and the code under the MIT licence. Please use the Ask panel only for questions about the data, and do not try to avoid its daily limit.

Questions and requests
