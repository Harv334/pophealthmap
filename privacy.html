About and privacy

PopHealth Map is an independent project run by Harvey Seville. It is not an NHS or government service, and it is not endorsed by the NHS, any council or any of the organisations whose data it shows.

The figures come from published official sources, which the methodology and technical notes list with their dates, while the way they are put together and presented is this project's own work.

Select any ward on the map to read a short summary, headed Summary, of how it compares with the other wards in its LA and its region.

In short

  • The site sets no cookies and has no analytics, no trackers and no adverts. Its fonts and map code are served from this site, not from Google or a code library service.
  • The map runs in your browser, on data files downloaded from this site. Selecting areas, filtering, comparing and exporting all happen on your own device.
  • Three parts of the map send something to another service. The background map does this whenever the map is open, because its street map or satellite images are loaded from CARTO or Esri, while postcode search and the Ask panel send something only when you use them. Each is described below.

The Ask panel

Please do not enter patient or personal information. Ask about places, not people: no names, NHS numbers, addresses or details of anyone's health or circumstances.

The Ask about this data panel, which is available on the London map for now, answers questions about the figures on the map. When you send a question:

  • Your question goes to this site's server, a small one that runs on Cloudflare, and the server passes it to Anthropic's Claude model, which writes the reply.
  • To write it, the model asks your browser for the figures it needs. Your browser looks them up in the map's own data, which it has already downloaded from this site, and the figures it finds pass back the same way, through this site's server to Anthropic. The model is never sent the rest of the data.
  • Everything said earlier in the panel goes along with each new question, so that the model can follow the conversation, and closing or reloading the page clears it.
  • You can ask 10 questions a day, and replying when the model asks you to narrow a question down does not use one up. A work network shared by many people also has a daily ceiling of its own, so it may run out sooner. To keep to these limits, the panel stores a random number in your browser that identifies nothing else and sends it with each question, and the server on Cloudflare records it and your IP address with a count of the questions and requests sent today. It deletes those records automatically after 48 hours.
  • If you never send a question, nothing is sent to that server or to Anthropic, and opening the panel on its own sends nothing.

The Ask panel is on the London map for now. On other regions and the England map it is not shown, and nothing is sent.

You can read more in Anthropic's privacy policy and Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Answers are written by an AI model. It is told to use only the figures it looks up in the map's own data, but it can still make mistakes, so check anything that matters before acting on it.

Postcode search

When you type a postcode into the search box, that postcode, and nothing else, is sent to postcodes.io, a free postcode lookup service, to find where it is. Like any website, postcodes.io also sees your IP address. Other searches, such as a ward, a GP practice or a charity, are matched in your browser and are not sent anywhere.

A full postcode can cover a small number of homes. If you are working from someone's address, search by ward or neighbourhood name instead.

The background map

The street map behind the data is made of image tiles requested from CARTO, or from Esri if CARTO's tiles cannot be loaded. If you switch the background to Satellite, the imagery and its place names come from Esri instead. As with any web request, the tile provider sees your IP address and which part of the map you are looking at. Nothing from the map's own data layers is sent with those requests.

Hosting

The pages and data files are served by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and the addresses of the pages and files your browser asks for. See GitHub's privacy statement.

What is stored in your browser

A few display preferences are kept in your browser's own storage so that the map opens the way you left it. They stay on your device and are not sent anywhere, and clearing this site's data in your browser removes them.

NameKept inPurpose
phTourSeenLocal storage Notes that the tour has been shown, so it does not start by itself on every visit.
phPanelCollapsedLocal storage Whether you hid the side panel on a wide screen.
phPanelWidthLocal storage The width you dragged the side panel to.
phMapControlsFoldedLocal storage Whether the map controls in the side panel are folded or open.

ph-england-okSession storage That you chose to open the map of all of England, which is a large download, so it does not ask again. Cleared when you close the tab.

Charity locations

The charities on the map and in the Directory come from the Charity Commission's register of charities, which publishes each charity's contact details. Many small charities give a trustee's home as their contact address, so for smaller charities the site shows only an approximate location and no street address.

Anyone can ask for a charity's record to be removed from this site by emailing contact@pophealth.uk with the charity's name or registered number. Changing the details on the register itself is done with the Charity Commission.

The data on the map

The statistics on the map are published figures for areas, such as wards and neighbourhoods, with the publishers' own rules for small numbers already applied. They are not records about individuals. GP practices, pharmacies, dentists and hospitals come from published NHS registers. The methodology and technical notes list every source.

Your personal data and your rights

Harvey Seville is responsible for the personal data this site handles. The only personal data the site keeps itself is your IP address and the Ask panel's random browser number, with a count of the Ask questions sent from them, which is deleted after 48 hours. We keep it because we have a legitimate interest in sharing the Ask panel fairly and keeping its cost within what the project can carry.

Cloudflare and Anthropic process your question for us as service providers, and either may handle it outside the UK, including in the United States, under the safeguards in their data processing terms. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, questions sent to its models this way are not used to train them, and Anthropic keeps them only for a limited time, mainly to check for misuse.

You can ask for a copy of any personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it, by emailing contact@pophealth.uk.

Using this site

PopHealth Map is provided free and as it is. The figures are published statistics about areas, brought together for planning, research and discussion. They are not medical advice and say nothing about any individual. We take care to reproduce each source faithfully, but we cannot promise that every figure is complete or current, and we are not responsible for decisions made using them. You may reuse the figures under the licences and with the credits listed on the methodology page, and the code under the MIT licence. Please use the Ask panel only for questions about the data, and do not try to get around its daily limit.
