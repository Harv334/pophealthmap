Accessibility statement

← Back to the map · PopHealth Map, London

This statement applies to pophealth.uk, an interactive map of population health across the 33 London boroughs. It covers the map itself, the methodology and technical notes, and this page.

How accessible this site is

Most of the site can be used with a keyboard and with a screen reader. The parts that cannot are listed below, in plain terms, with what to use instead.

These things work:

  • Every page can be zoomed to 200% without losing content or function.
  • The site follows your device's light or dark setting, and you can override it.
  • There is a colour-blind safe palette for the map's colours, on the CB safe button in the toolbar.
  • Colour is never the only way a figure is given. Everything shaded on the map is also written as a number in the panel and in the downloads.
  • Every control can be reached with the keyboard, and the one with focus is visibly outlined.
  • The tool strip and the sidebar tabs behave the way a screen reader is told they will: arrow keys move within them, and each is a single stop on the way through the page.
  • Selecting a ward, focusing a borough or a board, and zooming to an area are each announced to a screen reader.
  • There is a skip link to the main content on every page.

What is not accessible

The map's areas cannot be reached with a keyboard. The wards, neighbourhoods and boundaries are drawn as shapes that only respond to a mouse or a touch. There are about 1,400 of them on screen at once, and making each one a keyboard stop would put the rest of the page hundreds of presses away.

Everything the map can tell you is reachable another way:

  • The search box in the header finds a ward, a neighbourhood, a GP practice, a school or a charity by name or postcode. Choosing a result does exactly what clicking it on the map does.
  • Query lists the wards matching any set of conditions, as a list you can move through and select from.
  • The Directory holds every service and organisation on the map in one searchable, filterable list.
  • Data export writes the figures for every area to a spreadsheet, so nothing on the map is only on the map.

These are also known to fall short:

Building a custom area needs a mouse
A custom area is assembled by clicking neighbourhoods on the map, so it currently cannot be built with a keyboard. Once built it can be sent as a link, which does open for anyone. Data export covers the same figures for published areas without needing the map.
The map's own background is not described
Street names, rivers and parks come from OpenStreetMap and are drawn as images. They carry no text alternative. Nothing this site publishes depends on reading them.
Shading is described in words, not read out area by area
When the map is coloured by a figure, a screen reader is told what the colours mean and what the range is, but it will not read out all 704 wards. Use Query, the Top and Bottom 20 lists, or Data export to get that as an ordered list.
Some charts are images of their data
A few small bar and distribution graphics are drawn rather than written out. Every figure in them appears as a number nearby or in the ward's full profile.

Reporting accessibility problems

If you find a problem that is not listed here, or you need something on this site in a different format, email contact@pophealth.uk. Please say which page it was and what you were trying to do. We aim to reply within five working days.

If you ask for information in an accessible format and you are not happy with the response, say so in your reply and it will be looked at again.

Technical information

This site is partially compliant with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.2 AA standard, because of the exceptions listed above.

Non-accessible content

The content below is non-accessible for the following reasons.

  • The interactive map's areas are not operable by keyboard, which fails WCAG 2.2 success criterion 2.1.1 (keyboard). The alternatives listed above are provided under 1.1.1 as a text route to the same information. We are looking at whether a keyboard mode over a shortlist of areas can be added without making the rest of the page unusable.
  • Map tiles carry no text alternative, which fails success criterion 1.1.1 (non-text content). They are decorative context rather than data, and the data is available in text.

How this was tested

The site is checked with automated tests run in a real browser against the published pages. Those tests measure the contrast of the site's text colours in both light and dark themes against the WCAG formula, check that every control has a name a screen reader can announce, check that every image has a text alternative, and check that the page has one main heading, a main landmark and a language set. Keyboard order and screen-reader behaviour are checked by hand.

The site has not been through a formal external audit. This statement is written from the checks described above, and it names what those checks found rather than claiming more than they can show.

What we are doing to improve accessibility

  • Looking at a keyboard route into the map itself, most likely over a shortlist of areas rather than all 1,400 shapes.
  • Making the custom area builder usable without a mouse, by letting areas be added from a list as well as from the map.
  • Adding the automated checks above to the build, so a change that breaks one of them fails before it is published rather than after.
