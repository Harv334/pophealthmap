# Changelog

## Unreleased

These changes are written and tested, and reach the site with the next data refresh.

Twenty-five indicators that repeated others are taken off the map, and out of the Data export, Query, Compare, Ask and the postcode table. Of each pair the one published for the smaller area was kept, as its publisher puts it out. The ages and population now come only from the Census, which goes down to output areas, and say so in their names (Census 2021); the mid-year estimates went. Deprivation is the IMD score for wards and LAs and MHCLG's own decile for LSOAs; the average decile we worked out for wards went. Also gone: Non-White % (White % and the other groups stay), all adults having English as a main language, households deprived in at least one dimension, the IoD's adult skills and air quality indicators, PM10, total crimes (the crime rate stays), the IDACI, IDAOPI and SAMHI deciles, Local Health's claimant and fuel poverty copies, the LA series for life expectancy, healthy life expectancy, Year 6 obesity and children in low income families, and household income after tax before housing costs. Query's worked example now looks for wards with at least one Core20 LSOA.

The second indicator is taken off the map. Colouring the map by two indicators at once, in nine colours, was hard to read and hid the highest and lowest areas while it was on. Query, which lists the wards that meet several conditions with their figures, and Compare do the same job more clearly. A shared link that names a second indicator opens on the first one alone.

Public transport accessibility (PTAL) is taken off the map. The GLA's figures are from 2014, too old to show beside the current ones, and the map has its own up-to-date measures of the stations and bus stops near each area.

The GP-recorded prevalence estimates for MSOAs are on the map, for 19 conditions. They come from the House of Commons Library's GitHub copy of its figures, which is QOF 2019/20 and has not been updated since 2021, so the cards give the year and say how old the figures are. A current file from the Library's dashboard, attached by hand, replaces them. A file that names its conditions by their QOF register codes (AF, DM, STIA and so on) is now read. AHAH falls back to the GeoDS dataset page when GeoDS's record gives no download address, and says when the file has to be downloaded by hand.

Census percentages for wards and LAs are calculated from the summed counts of their output areas: the households with no car or van in a ward's output areas, for example, divided by all their households. A measure of households is therefore a share of households rather than an average weighted by residents. Each indicator card, the Area profile, the Data export notes and the methodology page describe whichever method built the figures on screen, so they change with the data.

Seventeen Census 2021 indicators are added, each for output areas, LSOAs, MSOAs, wards and LAs: households deprived in two or more dimensions and in at least one (from the household deprivation table, TS011), people not working because of long-term sickness or disability, people who have never worked or are long-term unemployed, unpaid care of 50 or more hours a week, people who cannot speak English well or at all, one-person households, people aged 66 and over living alone, lone parents with dependent children, overcrowded and under-occupied homes, and six detailed ethnic groups (Bangladeshi, Pakistani, Black African, Black Caribbean, Gypsy or Irish Traveller, and Roma). Household deprivation had been read from the wrong table (TS044, which is accommodation type), so no figure was ever made. Where ONS publishes less detail for output areas, as it may for spoken English, ward and LA figures are made from LSOA counts instead.

The routine and semi-routine jobs figure counted intermediate jobs and small employers as well, about twice the true share. It now counts semi-routine and routine occupations only, and is left out of the map, the reports and Ask until the refresh rebuilds it.

An indicator with no figures in the data that is loaded is no longer offered in the indicator picker or Ask, and Query, Compare and the Data export list only indicators with figures, so a new indicator appears once a refresh has made its figures. The card for OHID's out-of-work benefit measure now says how it differs from the claimant rate.

The Deprivation theme gains 24 measures from the English Indices of Deprivation 2025, for LSOAs, wards and LAs, in short lists of their own. They are income deprivation affecting children (IDACI) and older people (IDAOPI), each with its national decile; the six sub-domains of education, barriers to housing and services, and living environment; and 14 of the measures the index is built from, such as years of life lost to early deaths, emergency admissions, mood and anxiety disorders, Key Stage 2 and 4 results, entry to higher education, adults with no or low qualifications, housing in poor condition, air quality and road traffic accidents. A ward's or LA's IDACI is the share of its children aged 0 to 15, weighted by MHCLG's own counts of children, and IDAOPI is weighted the same way by people aged 60 and over. Ranks and deciles stay with LSOAs. The figures arrive with the next data refresh, which downloads the files from the release, and until then the measures are not offered. Any that the release turns out not to publish stays off the map, and the refresh says which. Ask's list of indicators is written more compactly, so it has room for them.

Six measures are added for small areas, each from the next data refresh, and until then they are not offered. The distance to the nearest GP practice, pharmacy and NHS dentist is measured for every LSOA in a straight line from the point ONS places where most of its people live, to the nearest site in England, so an LSOA near an LA boundary is measured to the practice over it; ward and LA figures are averages weighted by population. ONS's mid-year population estimates add a more recent population for LSOAs, wards and LAs, with the shares aged 0 to 15, 16 to 64, 65 and over and 75 and over, beside the Census figures rather than in place of them. Three are rates for wards and LAs made by adding up LSOA counts before dividing: the claimant rate among people aged 18 to 24, residents killed or seriously injured on the roads over five years per 1,000 residents (by where the casualty lives, using DfT's adjusted serious injuries), and care home beds registered with CQC per 100 people aged 75 and over. The crime figures, which were counted for wards, are counted for LSOAs too, so the crime rate, the total and the main types can be shown by LSOA; ward figures are unchanged. Each new source has its own entry in the update record, so one that fails does not hold back the others. Public transport travel times from the Urban Big Data Centre were looked at and left out: they are on 2011 LSOAs from a 2021 timetable, and the licence of the data could not be confirmed.

Three household income measures from ONS's income estimates for small areas, for the financial year ending 2023, are added for MSOAs, wards and LAs: average household income before tax, and after tax before and after housing costs, adjusted for household size. They are modelled estimates, so when the map shows MSOAs each figure has the range ONS is 95% sure it lies in beside it. Wards and LAs are averages of their MSOAs weighted by households.

ONS's healthy life expectancy and disability-free life expectancy at birth, for women and men, are added for MSOAs and wards, from deaths in 2019 to 2023 and the 2021 Census, each MSOA figure with its range. LA figures are ONS's own where the release has them, and otherwise our estimates from wards, which the key says.

Low birth weight of live babies and new cancer cases (all cancers, breast, bowel, lung and prostate, compared with England) are added from OHID's Local Health for MSOAs, wards and LAs. Each is shown only where OHID publishes figures for at least half of a region's MSOAs, with ranges narrow enough to tell areas apart; a series that is mostly withheld is left off the map. Breast and prostate cancer are not coloured as better or worse, because screening and testing raise them.

All of these arrive with the next data refresh, which finds the ONS files on their dataset pages, and until then they are not offered. The pipeline now takes indicators from OHID's other Fingertips profiles by name, from a list, rather than the first 40 of each profile in number order.

Thirteen measures of benefits from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) are added, read from its Stat-Xplore service, and they reach the map once the site owner has added a free Stat-Xplore key; until then they are not offered, and the update record says each is waiting for the key rather than failing. For LSOAs, wards and LAs: people on Universal Credit as a share of residents aged 16 to 64, households on Universal Credit as a share of all households, those with limited capability for work because of illness or disability (for a month a year before the latest, because assessments are backdated), and the share of households on Universal Credit with children. For wards and LAs: Personal Independence Payment claimants as a share of residents aged 16 and over, in all and for psychiatric, musculoskeletal and neurological conditions; children aged 0 to 15 receiving Disability Living Allowance; Attendance Allowance claimants as a share of residents aged 66 and over; Carer's Allowance claimants as a share of residents aged 16 and over; Pension Credit claims per 100 residents aged 66 and over; and children aged 0 to 15 in relative low income families, from DWP's own ward counts where its wards are the map's 2025 wards. Ward and LA figures add up the LSOAs' counts and their bases before dividing. DWP rounds or adjusts small counts, so each card says the figures for small areas are approximate. Each benefit has its own entry in the update record, so one that fails does not hold back the others.

A new Food and healthy places theme, and two more health measures, are added for small areas. GeoDS's healthy places index (Access to Healthy Assets and Hazards, AHAH, version 5.1) and its four domains (access to health services, how near fast food outlets, pubs, off-licences, gambling outlets and tobacconists are, air quality, and green and blue space) are added for LSOAs, wards and LAs, with the drive time in minutes by car to the nearest outlet of each of those five kinds, and a decile within England for LSOAs. PLDR's mental health index (SAMHI) is added the same way, with its decile. The House of Commons Library's estimates of illness recorded by GPs, for 20 conditions such as diabetes, asthma and depression, are added for MSOAs, wards and LAs. CDRC's risk of food insecurity (the Priority Places for Food Index) is added as deciles for LSOAs, five domains among them, and for wards and LAs as the share of residents living in the fifth of England at highest risk. From the Food Standards Agency's register, takeaways and sandwich shops, and pubs, bars and nightclubs, per 1,000 residents, and the share of rated food businesses with a hygiene rating of 0, 1 or 2, are added for wards and LAs. Each arrives with the next data refresh, the food insecurity figures once CDRC's file, which needs a login, has been added by hand, and until then none is offered. Files and reports with a food insecurity figure carry CDRC's attribution.

Ask's list of indicators leaves out each one's unit and period once it is long, and comes in pages if it is longer still, so it always fits in one answer; Ask can also search the list by words. Ask still gives the unit and period with every figure it looks up, and when a whole area's figures are too many to label one by one it sends them as short rows, with the labels in the list.

Ask is now on each region's map as well as London's. It is told which region's map is open, so it ranks and compares that region's own areas, and its example questions there name no places. The England map still has no Ask panel, because it holds too many areas to look up at once.

## 1.0.0, 16 October 2026

This is the first full release of PopHealth Map, a free map of population health with no sign-in. It puts small-area health, deprivation and Census figures on the same map as the GP practices, pharmacies and charities that serve those places, explains any area in plain English, and lets you build, compare and download your own areas. The site keeps its Beta badge, because the figures are still provisional while the site is being checked.

The map covers London in full, with regional packs for other parts of England and an England view. It draws every figure at the levels it can honestly be shown at, from the local authority (LA) down through wards, MSOAs and LSOAs to Census output areas of about 125 households, and the level switch offers only the levels each indicator supports. Every figure is placed on the 2021 Census areas first and then combined into wards and LAs with the official ONS lookups: counts are summed, and rates, including Census percentages, are weighted by population. Figures that are published only for a whole LA are labelled as such rather than repeated as though they described each ward.

The Indicators tab holds more than a hundred measures from open government and NHS sources. They include the Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025 and its domains, the Census 2021 topic summaries on health, disability, age, ethnicity, housing, tenure, work, qualifications, travel and unpaid care, OHID's Local Health and public health profiles, QOF disease prevalence, recorded crime, the claimant count, fuel poverty, air quality, green space and public transport access. Each indicator has a card that says what is counted and out of what, the level it is published at and how other levels are calculated from it, the period it covers, any caution about reading it, how it compares with England where the publisher says, and when it is next updated, with a link to its row on the methodology page. Two indicators can be shown together on one map, and the map colours a figure as better or worse only where it is clear which end is better.

The tools sit along the top of the sidebar. Explore is the map itself, with search by place, postcode, ward, LA or ICB. Query finds the wards that meet the conditions you set, Compare puts areas side by side, Custom area adds up any group of LSOAs you choose, and Data export downloads any level as a spreadsheet with a notes file that cites every source. Selecting an area opens its Area profile and a plain English summary, and any ward can be opened as a printable report. In London, Ask answers questions about the figures in plain English, using the same data the map shows.

The Layers tab adds GP practices, pharmacies, dentists, hospitals, primary care network catchments and green spaces, and the map can be shown over a street map or a satellite background. The Charities tab shows local voluntary and community organisations by cause, and the Directory lists practices, pharmacies and charities near a postcode or within a ward.

The site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 AA, and its accessibility statement lists where it still falls short. Everything the map shows can also be reached by keyboard through search, Query, the Directory and Data export, and the site works on phones and on desktops. Its methodology, accessibility and privacy pages explain how the figures are made, how the site can be used and what it does not collect. Every pull request and every deploy runs an automated browser test suite with accessibility checks on the map and the pages around it, and a check that no dash has crept into the text people read.
